## 1. Review purpose

This cross-review tests whether AC-103 describes the real Company-level customer lifecycle and value streams accurately enough to shape the later operating model, without confusing current client work with the future `«ИИ-компания под ключ»` product or duplicating product-specific workflows.

## 2. Review lenses

The review uses eleven functional perspectives:

1. Owner / Founder;
2. General Director;
3. Commercial / Sales;
4. Delivery / Operations;
5. Customer Success / Support;
6. Product / Portfolio;
7. Finance / Unit Economics;
8. Technology / Architecture;
9. Security / Data / Sovereignty;
10. Legal / Commitment Control;
11. Risk / Continuity.

These are review lenses only. They do not create Positions or delegated authority.

## 3. Iteration 1 — Evidence boundary: current work vs future flagship

**Primary lenses:** Owner, Product, Commercial, Architecture.

**Criticism:** The first plausible map risked treating the strategic flagship lifecycle as if it were already the Company's current customer lifecycle.

**Reconciliation:** The artifact now distinguishes:

- current bespoke client automation/delivery;
- prepared controlled-pilot patterns;
- standalone-product lifecycle as not yet sufficiently evidenced;
- future flagship AI-company transformation as a separate, explicitly future value stream.

**Result:** PASS after correction.

## 4. Iteration 2 — Customer/value-stream segmentation

**Primary lenses:** Commercial, Delivery, Product, Finance.

**Criticism:** One universal lifecycle would hide materially different value creation in bespoke delivery, controlled pilots and productization.

**Reconciliation:** Five value streams are separated:

- bespoke automation delivery;
- iterative correction to accepted outcome;
- controlled pilot / decision-support proof;
- productization from client work;
- future flagship transformation.

The review also records that repository existence alone does not prove standalone-product economics or a repeatable customer lifecycle.

**Result:** PASS.

## 5. Iteration 3 — Qualification, scope and commitment control

**Primary lenses:** Owner, General Director, Commercial, Legal, Finance.

**Criticism:** A lifecycle that begins at implementation omits where bad work enters the system: weak qualification, ambiguous scope and uncontrolled commitment.

**Reconciliation:** The common lifecycle now begins with opportunity/request → discovery/scoping → commitment before build/configure. Explicit control points require later accountability for qualification, promised outcome/exclusions, price/scope/time/support commitments and customer-input readiness.

No future Sales Position is invented.

**Result:** PASS.

## 6. Iteration 4 — Delivery, customer validation, rework and acceptance

**Primary lenses:** Delivery, Customer Success, Risk, Finance.

**Criticism:** Current client work shows that value is often created through correction after real customer validation. Treating all rework as failure would be false; treating all rework as included scope would be economically dangerous.

**Reconciliation:** The map explicitly distinguishes:

- internal verification;
- customer validation;
- targeted correction/re-delivery;
- acceptance/handover;
- later support/continuation.

The artifact identifies open-ended iteration as a structural risk and records the future need to distinguish defect, agreed scope and new change request.

**Result:** PASS.

## 7. Iteration 5 — Customer outcome and commercial-value evidence

**Primary lenses:** Customer Success, Commercial, Finance, Owner.

**Criticism:** Current repositories are much stronger at proving technical completion than at proving customer economic outcome.

**Reconciliation:** The baseline states explicitly that `works / delivered / accepted` does not prove hours saved, revenue created, error reduction, adoption or willingness to pay. Prospective engagements should capture customer-confirmed outcome/usefulness and management-level revenue/margin class where needed.

This is carried into AC-107/AC-108 and later operating evidence; AC-103 does not fabricate historical KPIs.

**Result:** PASS.

## 8. Iteration 6 — Reuse, customer rights and OS/Product boundary

**Primary lenses:** Product, Architecture, Security, Legal.

**Criticism:** The learning/productization stream could accidentally imply that customer-specific data, knowledge or authority may be reused across clients, or that repeated product logic automatically belongs in Arvectum OS.

**Reconciliation:** The artifact now requires explicit separation of customer-specific work, product capability, reusable module candidates and OS-domain-neutral capability. Customer data/knowledge/history and Organizational Authority remain customer-scoped; technical processing creates no reuse right by implication.

**Result:** PASS.

## 9. Iteration 7 — Owner bottleneck and handoff to AC-104

**Primary lenses:** Owner, Operations, Risk, all remaining lenses.

**Criticism:** A lifecycle map that does not expose where the Owner carries context and exceptions would be insufficient for the next organizational-design step.

**Reconciliation:** The artifact identifies likely Owner concentration in:

- opportunity judgment;
- ambiguous discovery/scoping;
- commitment decisions;
- prioritization/technical decomposition;
- customer expectation management;
- exception/rework review;
- acceptance/closure judgment;
- reuse/productization decisions.

This is deliberately recorded as a hypothesis to test in AC-104 rather than a pre-decided organizational structure.

**Result:** PASS.
